# OMNIVO Website Copy & SEO Plan

This document is the approved copy baseline for OMNIVO’s four website pages. Published page text, SEO metadata, hero and section images, banners, and projects are managed in the Django CMS. The user-confirmed service list is: custom software, AI agent development, web application, mobile application, and SQA / AI testing. “Customer Software” was interpreted as **Custom Software**; confirm the intended service name before launch if that interpretation is wrong.

## Content strategy

The site is written for business and product decision-makers who need software built or tested. Its purpose is to help a visitor identify the relevant service, understand the questions that affect scope, and start a useful project enquiry. It does not claim a target country, industry specialization, customer history, certification, technology stack, prices, delivery times, or performance results because those facts were not provided.

The copy follows a people-first approach: answer evaluation questions, explain trade-offs, and use clear service names instead of repeating search terms. Research suggested the same useful distinction across the five categories: custom software is a build-versus-configure decision; AI agent work needs boundaries for data, actions, review and exceptions; web and mobile projects have different user and platform considerations; and SQA / AI testing should communicate evidence and limitations rather than promise zero defects.

The current implementation omits canonical URLs, `og:url`, absolute social-image URLs, and a sitemap until OMNIVO supplies its real production domain. Meta keywords are included for the project’s existing SEO checklist, but they are not a substitute for useful page content or search performance.

## Home page copy

**Eyebrow:** Software for real business needs

**H1:** Custom software, AI agents & apps for real work.

**Hero paragraph:** OMNIVO develops custom software, AI agents, web and mobile applications, and SQA/AI testing. Start with a business or product need; choose the technology only after the job is clear.

**Primary CTA:** Discuss your project

**Secondary CTA:** Explore capabilities

**Hero support line:** Start with the work. Define the need before choosing a build path.

**Service cards:**

- **Custom software:** For a workflow with a clear gap that existing products may not fit.
- **AI agent development:** For context-heavy tasks where actions and human review need clear boundaries.
- **Web applications:** For browser-based tools shaped around specific users and journeys.
- **Mobile applications:** For product experiences where the device and moment of use matter.
- **SQA & AI testing:** For teams that need clearer evidence about software and AI behavior.

**Section: Choose a starting point. Keep the goal in view.** Each capability solves a different kind of product or process challenge. Use the service guide to compare fit and scope.

**Section: Don’t start with a tool. Start with the task.** A custom build is not automatically better than adapting an existing product. An AI agent is not automatically better than straightforward automation. Compare the workflow, exceptions, current systems and risks before committing to an approach.

Decision prompts: **Workflow** — What needs to change? **Fit** — Build, adapt or automate? **Evidence** — How will it be evaluated?

**Section: Three details make the first chat clearer.** You do not need a finished specification. A few real-world details are enough to begin discussing fit.

1. **The work that is difficult today:** Describe the step, delay, hand-off, or product gap you want to change.
2. **The people and systems involved:** Note who uses the process and which tools, platforms, or data it touches.
3. **The evidence you would value:** Think about what should work differently and what would help you assess it.

**Brand principle:** Choose technology for the task it needs to do—not because it is new. A useful starting principle for custom software, AI agents, applications and testing.

**Closing CTA:** Have a defined challenge or an early idea? Bring the problem. We’ll help scope the question.

## Services page copy

**Eyebrow:** Five capabilities, different jobs

**H1:** Software development for real business needs.

**Hero paragraph:** Compare custom software, AI agent development, web and mobile applications, and SQA/AI testing. Each section outlines a different use case and the questions worth settling before work begins.

### Custom software development

**Lead:** When a workflow needs a better fit.

Custom software may suit a business process that existing products cannot support well. The first decision is not simply what to build: compare configuration, integration, and a purpose-built tool against the actual workflow, data, users, constraints, and ongoing ownership.

That comparison helps define what the software must do, which systems it needs to work with, and how its behavior should be evaluated.

**Scope prompts:** Workflow and requirements definition; purpose-built business software; integration and data considerations; testing and ownership questions.

**CTA:** Talk through a custom build.

### AI agent development

**Lead:** For context-heavy workflows.

An AI agent can use context and connected tools to work through a defined task. Whether an agent is a sensible fit depends on the workflow: predictable, rule-based steps may be simpler to automate without an agent.

For an agent use case, scope the information it may use, permitted actions, points for human approval, and what should happen when the task falls outside expectations. Those boundaries matter as much as the model choice.

**Scope prompts:** Use-case fit versus conventional automation; information and tool-access boundaries; human review and exception paths; evaluation criteria for intended tasks.

**CTA:** Assess an agent use case.

### Web application development

**Lead:** For browser-based products and tools.

A web application gives customers or staff a browser-based way to complete tasks, work with information, or use a digital product. Planning starts with the people using it and the journey they need to complete—not a list of screens alone.

Scope can then account for user roles, data, connected services, access needs, accessibility expectations, and how the application will be tested and maintained.

**Scope prompts:** User roles and task flows; application features and priorities; integration and access requirements; accessibility and quality considerations.

**CTA:** Scope a web application.

### Mobile application development

**Lead:** For work that happens on a device.

A mobile application can make sense when a customer or employee needs a dedicated experience on a phone or tablet. The right scope depends on the use case, intended users, target platforms and devices, connectivity, and any device-specific requirements.

Deciding platform coverage and integration needs early gives the project a clearer basis for design, development, and testing.

**Scope prompts:** Mobile user journeys and product scope; iOS and Android coverage to be agreed; device and integration requirements; testing and release responsibilities.

**CTA:** Plan a mobile product.

### Software quality assurance & AI testing

**Lead:** For clearer release decisions.

Software quality assurance (SQA) helps teams examine whether a product behaves as expected across the scenarios that matter to its users and release. The useful scope depends on product risks, acceptance expectations, and the decision the testing should inform.

AI testing adds questions about variable outputs and behavior across different inputs. Testing can provide evidence about observed behavior; it cannot promise that every defect or risk has been found.

**Scope prompts:** Test scope shaped around product risks; expected behavior and scenario coverage; additional evaluation for AI features; findings and limitations for review.

**CTA:** Discuss testing scope.

### Services FAQ

**Is custom software always better than an existing product?** No. If configuration or integration of an existing tool meets the need, it may be a more suitable route. A custom build is worth evaluating when important workflow requirements remain unmet. Compare the options against users, constraints, integration needs, and long-term ownership.

**When might an AI agent be worth exploring?** Consider an agent when a workflow relies on context, unstructured information, or variable exceptions. If the steps are predictable, conventional automation may be easier to control. Any AI use case should define data access, allowed actions, review points, and evaluation criteria.

**What is the difference between a web application and a custom software project?** “Web application” describes a browser-based way to use software. “Custom software” describes a solution designed around a particular need. A custom web app can be both; the right format depends on users, workflows, devices, and operating constraints.

**Can software or AI testing guarantee a defect-free release?** No test plan can establish that every possible defect has been found. Testing can examine agreed scenarios and provide evidence about what was observed, what remains uncertain, and which issues may need attention before a release decision.

## About page copy

**Eyebrow:** The problem comes first

**H1:** Good products begin with clear questions.

**Hero paragraph:** OMNIVO works across software, AI, applications, and testing. The useful first step is to understand the work a product must do, the people it affects, and the constraints a solution needs to respect.

**Section: A strong brief explains the problem behind the feature.** That distinction creates room to compare approaches before a choice becomes expensive to change. It also gives the people involved a clearer way to discuss scope, assumptions, and what evidence would make the result useful.

**Section: Four questions keep scope honest.**

- **What should change?** Describe the user or business task that is difficult today and the difference a solution should make.
- **What already exists?** Current software, data, policies, and team routines shape what can be built or connected.
- **Where are the boundaries?** Clarify access, approvals, platform needs, exceptions, and what should remain under human control.
- **What would count as evidence?** Agree how expected behavior, test findings, and open questions will inform a decision.

**Section: Make the important decisions visible.** A practical project scope records what is known, what still needs an answer, and how choices will be made.

1. **Frame the need:** Set the intended users, workflow, and problem in plain language.
2. **Compare the options:** Consider building, adapting, connecting, or automating against the real constraints.
3. **Agree how to assess it:** Define expected behavior and the evidence that will support review.

**Brand principle:** Good technology decisions make trade-offs visible. Scope, risk, assumptions, and open questions belong in the conversation.

**Closing CTA:** The right scope can start small. Bring the question you’re working through.

## Contact page copy and enquiry form

**Eyebrow:** Start with the work

**H1:** What needs to work better?

**Hero paragraph:** Tell us about the task, product idea, or quality question you’re dealing with. You can leave the technical solution open; the first useful step is understanding the need.

**Form reassurance:** A partial brief is fine. Share what is known and flag what is still uncertain.

**Helpful context:** Users, workflow, current tools, and constraints. For AI projects: information, actions, review points, and exceptions. For testing projects: product risks and the decision testing should inform.

**Form fields:** Name (required); email address (required); company or team (optional); service area (optional: custom software development, AI agent development, web application development, mobile application development, SQA / AI testing); project stage (optional: early idea, planning scope, existing product or workflow, quality or testing question, exploring options); project description (required).

**Project description prompt:** What is difficult today? Who is affected? Mention current tools, key constraints, or how you would assess a better result.

**Submit label:** Prepare project enquiry.

**Static form disclosure:** This static preview does not send or store submissions. Connect a form endpoint and publish a privacy notice before collecting live enquiries.

The service links on the Services page preselect the relevant service area on this form. The form validates required fields in the browser and preserves the draft, but it is not connected to an inbox or CRM.

## Non-repetition and conversion design

- **Home** introduces the five categories with one-sentence summaries, then answers the build/adapt/automate decision and gives visitors useful briefing prompts.
- **Services** is the only page with full service descriptions, scope considerations, and service-specific questions.
- **About** focuses on how to frame and evaluate a project; it does not repeat the service catalogue or make up a founder story, company age, client list, or credentials.
- **Contact** is an intake page. It asks for context and qualifies an enquiry without repeating the service explanations.
- The shared footer and navigation are intentionally consistent sitewide; the page-specific body copy is distinct.
- Each service CTA includes a service identifier in the URL so the contact form can preselect that service.

## Market and content research notes

This is a qualitative content assessment, not keyword-volume research or a claim about OMNIVO’s actual customer base. The service angles reflect common evaluation questions in the technical guidance reviewed: feasibility and ownership for custom software; task fit, access, actions and human oversight for AI agents; usability, accessibility and system constraints for apps; and risk-based evidence for QA and AI testing. The content avoids treating standards as certifications or evidence of OMNIVO compliance.

Before publishing, OMNIVO should confirm that its actual project practice can support the process and scope statements on the About and Services pages. Add real team details, portfolio examples, customer evidence, industries, locations served, service terms, delivery model, or contact channels only after they are supplied and approved. Do not fabricate them to fill design space.
